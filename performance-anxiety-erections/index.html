Why Can I Get Hard Alone but Lose It During Sex?

You can get a strong erection when you’re alone. You feel turned on, everything works, and staying hard feels easy.

Then you’re with someone you actually want to have sex with.

You start getting hard. Things are going well. Suddenly, your erection starts fading.

Now you’re thinking about it.

Why am I getting soft? Is she noticing? What if I can’t get hard again?

The more you think about your erection, the harder it becomes to stay focused on what you’re feeling.

This pattern can happen when anxiety, pressure, or distractions interfere with sexual arousal. Physical health factors can also contribute, even when your erections are strong during masturbation.

Understanding when and how it happens can help you figure out what to investigate.

1. Why your erections may be different with a partner

When you’re alone, you usually control the experience.

You choose the stimulation, the pace, and what you focus on. You can change positions or slow down whenever you want.

During partnered sex, more things compete for your attention.

You might be thinking about:

  • Whether your partner is enjoying herself.
  • How long your erection will last.
  • Whether you’re performing well.
  • What happened the last time you lost your erection.

These thoughts can create pressure.

Anxiety can interfere with the processes involved in sexual arousal. When your attention shifts toward monitoring your erection, it may become harder to stay engaged with pleasurable sensations.

You may notice yourself repeatedly checking whether you’re still hard.

That checking can become part of the problem.

2. How one bad experience can affect the next

Imagine losing your erection during sex with someone you’re excited about.

The experience bothers you.

A few days later, you have another opportunity to have sex.

Before anything happens, you remember what went wrong.

You start wondering whether it’ll happen again.

During foreplay, you’re already paying attention to your erection. Every small change in firmness makes you more concerned.

This can create a cycle:

Previous difficulty → Worry about the next encounter → Increased self-monitoring → Difficulty staying aroused → More worry

Anxiety can contribute to erection difficulties, and erection difficulties can increase anxiety.

Recognizing this pattern gives you something specific to address.

3. What your erections during masturbation can tell you

Think about when your erections are strongest.

  • Can you get fully hard when you’re alone?
  • Do you wake up with erections?
  • Do you lose firmness during partnered sex consistently, or only in certain situations?

These details help healthcare professionals understand possible contributing factors.

Strong erections during masturbation or sleep may suggest that situational or psychological factors play a role.

They cannot rule out a physical cause.

Erection quality also depends on circulation, nerve function, hormones, medication effects, and general health.

Conditions such as diabetes and high blood pressure can affect erectile function.

If the problem continues, a medical evaluation can help identify underlying causes.

4. What to try during your next sexual encounter

Start by giving yourself a chance to experience intimacy with less pressure.

Step 1: Slow down

Spend more time on kissing, touching, and other enjoyable activities.

Give yourself time to become aroused.

Step 2: Bring your attention back to sensation

Notice what you’re physically experiencing.

Pay attention to your partner’s touch, your breathing, and what feels pleasurable.

When you catch yourself checking your erection, gently return your attention to those sensations.

Step 3: Let your breathing settle

If you notice yourself becoming tense, try breathing slowly and comfortably.

Relax your shoulders, jaw, and abdomen.

Breathing exercises can help with general relaxation. They don’t guarantee an erection.

Step 4: Communicate with your partner

If you feel comfortable, explain that you’re working through some erection inconsistency.

You can continue enjoying intimacy without putting pressure on yourself to immediately achieve penetration.

Step 5: Review what happened afterward

Think about the experience.

  • When did you feel most aroused?
  • When did you start feeling pressure?
  • What helped you feel more comfortable?

Look for patterns across several experiences.

5. Pay attention to your health outside the bedroom

Your daily habits can affect erectile function.

Consider your sleep, physical activity, alcohol use, smoking, and overall health.

Cardiovascular health is especially relevant because erections depend on healthy blood flow.

Certain medications, including some antidepressants and blood pressure treatments, may also affect sexual function. Speak with your prescriber before changing any medication.

Improving general health may support erection quality over time.

6. Try This: Your 7-Day Erection Consistency Check

Section 3 asks you to think about when your erections are strongest. Most men answer from memory, and memory tends to collect the bad nights.

This is a way to answer it from observation instead.

It takes about a minute a day for seven days. You need paper or the notes app already on your phone. There is nothing to buy, nothing to sign up for, and no personal information to hand over to anyone, including us. Nobody else needs to see it.

Each day, write down five things

  1. SleepRoughly how many hours, and whether it felt restful.
  2. StressOne word for the day, plus anything specific that weighed on you.
  3. Morning erectionFull, partial, or none that you noticed on waking.
  4. SituationAlone, with a partner, or no sexual activity. If there was, what your erection was like and whether it changed.
  5. WorryWhether you caught yourself checking or thinking about your erection, and at what point it started.

Keep each entry to a line. The value is in having seven days side by side, not in writing a lot on any one of them.

After seven days, read it back

Lay the week out and look for four things:

  • Do morning erections show up on most days, some days, or none of them?
  • Do the nights with less sleep or more stress line up with the days you noticed a difference?
  • Does the difference appear in every situation, or only with a partner?
  • When does the worry start: before anything happens, or after you notice a change?

A pattern that shows up only with a partner, on a week of normal sleep and ordinary stress, points somewhere different from a week with few morning erections and a difference in every situation.

Either way, you now have seven days of specifics to bring to section 7.

What this check cannot do. Seven days of notes cannot diagnose the cause of erection difficulties. It is not a test, it does not rule anything in or out, and a clear-looking week is not a reason to delay an appointment you were already considering.

What it can do is replace a vague worry with a description. A healthcare professional asks when the difficulty happens, how often, and what is going on around it. Those are the five things you just spent a week writing down, so take the notes with you.

7. When should you see a doctor?

Arrange a medical evaluation if you’re repeatedly having trouble getting or maintaining an erection.

Medical assessment is particularly important when:

  • Your morning erections have become noticeably less frequent.
  • You’re also having difficulty getting hard during masturbation.
  • You have diabetes, high blood pressure, or cardiovascular risk factors.
  • The change happened suddenly or is becoming more frequent.
  • You experience penile pain, curvature, or other physical changes.

A healthcare professional can review your symptoms, medication history, blood pressure, and relevant laboratory results.

Counseling or sex therapy may also help when anxiety contributes to the problem.

9. Your next step

Pay attention to where your erection difficulties happen, how often they occur, and what you’re experiencing when they begin.

Start with one practical exercise:

During your next intimate experience, focus on physical sensation and comfortable breathing. Notice when your attention shifts toward checking your erection.

Use what you learn to guide your next steps.
